For 24 hours on October 10–11, 95 students took over USC's Molinaroli College of Engineering and Computing to build something from scratch. PalmettoHacks 2026 was hosted by Kappa Theta Pi at USC and presented by the Boyd Innovation Center. It was also the University of South Carolina's first official Major League Hacking (MLH) event, and it showed exactly what a Columbia, SC hackathon can look like when you put talented students in a room with a deadline.

### PalmettoHacks 2026 by the numbers

- 95 students checked in
- 6 universities represented
- 24 teams submitted projects
- 10 track winners
- Over $3,000 in prizes

### A Columbia SC hackathon that drew students from across the state and beyond

Students came from USC, Benedict College, Clemson, Claflin, Winthrop, and Carnegie Mellon. The event was free, meals were included, and it was open to any major at any experience level. Plenty of people showed up without a team or an idea and left with both.

### What students built in 24 hours

A lot of the projects took on problems close to home. One team designed a drone delivery network to move urgent items across Columbia faster. Another went after something every Columbia driver knows too well: trains blocking roads, including for emergency vehicles. Other teams built tools to help clinicians catch patient care risks, inspect utility poles with computer vision, and run the back office for home-service businesses.

Teams competed across tracks including Power the Future (sponsored by Dominion Energy), Grid Modernization (sponsored by SCRA), Best Use of Hardware, a set of MLH technology challenges, and Most Startup Ready, sponsored by the Boyd Innovation Center. You can browse every submission in the [PalmettoHacks project gallery on Devpost](https://palmettohacks-2026.devpost.com/project-gallery).

### Symphony wins the BIC's Most Startup Ready track

We sponsored the Most Startup Ready track because a great hackathon project can be the first version of a real company. We were looking for the team with a real problem, a clear customer, and a product that could keep going after the weekend ended.

That team was **Symphony**. Ty Bennett, Andrew Heuer, Nikhil Krishna, and Sebastian Boscan-Villalobos built efficient, private, local AI for small businesses. It's a practical take on AI that keeps data in the business's own hands. Congratulations to the Symphony team, and to every team that shipped a project.
