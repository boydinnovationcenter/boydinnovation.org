## What if you could test a startup idea in 24 hours, for free?

That’s the premise behind Startup Sprint. On November 13–14, nearly 30 people showed up at the Boyd Innovation Center to find out. Students, professionals and community members, most of them strangers to each other, came with one thing in common: curiosity about entrepreneurship and a willingness to build.

### Friday: Pitches and teams

After an icebreaker (and a few random questions answered under pressure), everyone did a short writing exercise and decided whether to pitch an idea or join someone else’s. Then our idea-bringers took the mic for one minute each to recruit a team. By the end of the night, five teams had formed.

**The sprint was officially on.**

### Saturday: Seven sprint blocks, one day of building

After coffee and breakfast, teams worked through seven expert-led blocks:

1. **Problem & Solution Framing:** Dana Watkins, Founder, [Snaply Sites](https://snaplysites.com/)
2. **Customer Personas & Journey:** Mary Kate Korpita, VP of Client Engagement, [Flock & Rally](https://www.flockandrally.com/)
3. **Validation:** Tony Stephenson, Founder, [NautiQ](https://nautiq.ai/)
4. **What Is an MVP?** Sam Herskovitz, BIC Executive Director and former Product Manager
5. **Building Prototypes with AI:** Lucie DeCristofaro, Co-Founder, Winslo
6. **Refining Your Solution:** Ron Tryon, Co-Founder, [Clutch Intelligence](https://clutchintelligence.com/)
7. **The Power Pitch:** Cynthia Villar, Director, CIU’s Business Career Development Center

Between sessions, block leaders moved from team to team giving individual feedback. Less lecture, more hands-on coaching from people who’ve built real businesses.

### The pitches

By late afternoon, teams pitched to a panel of judges ([Jade Pruett](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jade-pruett-helloseo/), [Al Moseley](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alton-moseley/), [Luke Bare](https://www.linkedin.com/in/luke-bare-35358524a/) and [Luke Jannazzo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lukejannazzo/)) who asked tough questions and scored each concept on innovation, viability and scalability.

- **Most Innovative:** Vendor Intel
- **Most Viable:** Dr. G’s Migraine Magician
- **Most Scalable:** StandUp AI

Each winning team earned three free months of BIC community membership. But every team did the same remarkable thing: walked in as strangers and walked out with a validated, prototyped and pitched idea.

### Why it matters

Participants left with new connections (maybe even co-founders), hands-on experience with each step of building a startup, and a clearer sense of whether to keep going, pivot or join someone else’s idea.

That’s the point. The BIC exists to help founders build scalable technology companies in the greater Columbia area, and that starts with making the first leap feel less lonely. **That’s what we’re building here.**
