Every startup begins with a problem, but for Yuri and Svitlana, the co-founders of [**NeedNoMore**](https://neednomore.com/), that problem was deeply personal, painful, and persistent.

NeedNoMore is a platform that helps people facing food insecurity find food pantries and other essential resources. More than a map, their mobile app gives users accurate, real-time information and a voice. It’s available nationwide, and users can request new markets.

NeedNoMore is a social entrepreneurship example: a startup built to solve a social problem its founders lived through. After relying on food pantries across four states, Yuri and Svitlana built and launched a mobile app with a community-updated, real-time map of food pantries, so people facing food insecurity stop wasting trips to pantries that are closed, full, or outside their area.

## **Navigating a Broken System**

[Yuri and Svitlana came to the United States from Ukraine](https://neednomore.com/about) two years ago. For a year and a half, they relied heavily on food pantries to survive, moving through four states. During this time, they repeatedly encountered a widespread and tragic pattern: there was too much information about resources, but only about one-third of that information was accurate, by the founders’ estimate.

Svitlana vividly recalled a day when she desperately needed food for herself and her hungry, crying baby. She visited four food pantries in a row, only to be rejected at each one. Reasons included a pantry being closed without notice, not serving her zip code, or being at full capacity despite people leaving with bags of fresh food, including strawberries she hadn't seen in months. She returned to her car, overwhelmed by "desperation and hopelessness".

This experience, along with the painful realization that they were often receiving rotten, spoiled, or expired food with no voice to complain, fueled their mission. NeedNoMore is built on the belief that to be underprivileged doesn't mean to be disrespected or to stay silent. They built a solution where users gain control over their life by having a voice and a choice.

## **How Does NeedNoMore’s Real-Time Pantry Map Work?**

Yuri and Svitlana [built a mobile app](https://neednomore.com/) that changes how people find food assistance. At its core is a real-time map of food pantries, now live in markets across the country.

The community drives the platform:

- Users update the data. Anyone can correct a pantry’s hours, location, or status.
- Updates appear in real time. When one user reports a pantry closed today, every other user sees it immediately.
- Fewer wasted trips. People check before they travel, so they don’t arrive at a closed or moved location.

Svitlana’s morning shows what bad pantry data costs: four trips, four rejections, and no way to know before she arrived. NeedNoMore built the tool that would have told her.

## **Launchpad COLA: The Catalyst for Building and Refining**

Yuri and Svitlana brought this urgent, mission-driven idea to the Boyd Innovation Center (BIC) and the [Launchpad COLA Incubator,](/programs) a 12-week program designed by [Launchpad GVL](https://launchpadgvl.com/) to help founders turn tech ideas into real businesses.

Joining the program requires significant commitment. To attend the 7 AM Thursday sessions at the BIC, Yuri and Svitlana wake up well before dawn, requiring a working system with a nanny to care for their young child, since they leave when it is dark.

Despite the early start, the effort is paying off. They are highly motivated because they get so much from the program every week. Their product is changing and growing, and their vision is expanding. They rely on the Launchpad cohort, a community of builders, as an invaluable "oracle of knowledge". When they have a question, three or four people immediately brainstorm with them, providing insights that they estimate would take them "weeks or years" to come to by themselves.

Mentorship has shaped the product. They appreciate their mentor's candor, noting he "doesn't hold back". By challenging them with questions they hadn't asked themselves, their mentor is helping them rapidly improve their product.

Their experience reflects the program's philosophy that it's better to work in community than it is to work alone.

## What Makes NeedNoMore a Social Entrepreneurship Example?

Social entrepreneurs build businesses to solve social problems. NeedNoMore fits that definition in three ways: its founders experienced food insecurity firsthand, its product targets a specific failure in the system (inaccurate pantry information), and its users supply the data that keeps the map accurate. The team plans to work with municipalities, health organizations, donors, and food banks to reach the people who need it most.
